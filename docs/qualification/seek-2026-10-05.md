# Seek qualification, 2026-10-05

Follow-up to [performance-2026-10-04](performance-2026-10-04.md). Its run 3
failed warm seek at 1080p and 4K, and spent 6.9–15.9 s decoding the whole
Original before a cold first picture. This record covers four changes:
threaded serving decoders, gated non-reference preroll skipping,
single-threaded index measurement and progressive preview admission.

It supersedes run 3 for seek, playback picture cost and export only. These are
engineering measurements on one machine, not release qualification.

Two runs are reported:

- Run 4 measured an earlier revision of this work, in which measurement also
  used 16 threads and skipping was ungated. It is kept only for playback and
  export, which the review changes do not affect.
- Run 5 is the reviewed design and is authoritative for seek.

## What the time was spent on

- Single-threaded decode. The native adapter forced `thread_count = 1`.
  Whole-file decode with the Homebrew FFmpeg CLI (not the pinned build) took
  6.5/12.3/13.4 s single-threaded and 1.1–1.2 s with automatic threads for
  Caminandes, 1080p60 and 4K30. The VideoToolbox hwaccel path took
  10.9/21.9/9.9 s, slower than threaded software, so it was not pursued.
- Preroll. A random seek decodes from the indexed keyframe: Caminandes
  keyframes are up to 128 frames apart, the generated fixtures 250. RGBA
  conversion already ran only for the target. In these x264 streams, about
  half the preroll pictures are non-reference B pictures that nothing depends
  on. All four real fixtures declare `bitstream_restriction` and
  `frame_mbs_only_flag`, so gated skipping applies to them.
- Cold admission measured the full index before the first picture. Store and
  plan open in 10–19 ms. Two verified copies of the original plus decoder open
  take 90 ms (55 MB), 190 ms (126 MB) and 240 ms (158 MB).

Interactive serving decoders use one thread per core, capped at 12
(`MAX_INTERACTIVE_DECODE_THREADS`). That clears 80 ms at 1080p with margin,
for less memory than 16. Import qualification, export and other offline
consumers default to one thread.

## Design after review

- **Threading.** `DecodeLimits::threads` (1–16, default 1) enables FFmpeg
  frame and slice threading for serving decoders only.
  - Codec callbacks may run on codec threads after a call returns. They read
    only immutable limits and report through an atomic code, so the borrowed
    cancellation pointer is never touched off the caller thread.
  - A replacement codec clears that code.
- **Measurement.** Every index is measured with one codec thread: at
  registration, in complete admission and in the background. Receipts
  therefore never depend on the serving thread count.
  - Complete admission measures with a single-threaded decoder, then opens
    the threaded serving decoder.
- **Skipping.** `seek_to` skips non-reference pictures presented before the
  target only while every SPS (AVC configuration and in-band) declares
  `bitstream_restriction` and `frame_mbs_only_flag`.
  - Without a declared reorder depth, FFmpeg estimates it from the pictures
    it sees, and skipping changes those. Field coding pairs pictures across
    packets.
  - Otherwise every preroll picture decodes.
- **Per-picture checks.** Every decoded preroll picture and the target
  compare PTS and reported duration with the index. Both come from the
  picture's own packet.
  - The key flag and decode timestamp are not compared. H.264 reports the DTS
    of the packet that released a reordered picture, and recovery-point key
    marking depends on where decoding started.
- **Progressive admission.** It adopts the receipt index, shared through
  `Arc` rather than copied, and serves at once.
  - A utility-QoS single-threaded background decoder compares the fresh
    measurement entry by entry, without materializing a second index.
  - `Mismatch` is permanent: the native worker keeps a tombstone session,
    and every request fails with "Source verification failed: …" without
    re-copying.
  - `Interrupted` (deadline, cancellation, I/O) makes the worker drop the
    session and admit once more in the same request.
  - The background deadline is 24 hours. Dropping the session cancels and
    joins it within one single-threaded codec call, so no measurement
    outlives its session. Drop-to-return took under 1 s in tests.

## Identity evidence

- `native/deadpan-source/tests/threaded_seek.rs`:
  - Sequential decode at 2, 8 and 16 threads equals single-threaded decode by
    BLAKE3 for six fixtures. These include an x264 B-pyramid with reference
    B pictures (`pyramid-bframes.mp4`), a `frame_mbs_only_flag` 0 stream
    (`fake-interlaced.mp4`) and FFV1.
  - Every frame of five H.264 fixtures, as a backward `seek_to` target at 1
    and 8 threads plus two forward steps, equals the sequential decode.
  - Counting codec-allocated pictures, skipping decodes fewer for the pyramid
    fixture and the same number for the VideoToolbox fixtures, which lack
    `bitstream_restriction`, and for `fake-interlaced.mp4`.
  - Interlaced FFV1 is rejected at every thread count. Codec-thread format
    rejection keeps `unsupported_depth`.
- A development probe on long files compared full RGBA hashes and metadata,
  DTS and key flags included, between single-threaded and 12-thread
  sequential decodes. It also compared 150 random threaded `seek_to` targets
  plus two forward steps each. Files: interview (595 frames), Caminandes
  (3,504), gen-1080p60 (7,200), gen-4k30 (1,800) and a 300-frame 1080p
  16-slice FFV1 file. Result: 0 mismatches.
- `crates/deadpan-media/tests/progressive_admission.rs` covers:
  - progressive pictures equal complete admission;
  - a tampered receipt entry is never served, and its `Mismatch` fails
    every later request;
  - an exhausted 1 ns background deadline is `Interrupted`, and a new
    admission verifies;
  - complete-admission indexes are identical at 1, 2 and 16 serving threads;
  - receipt identity and metadata are checked at open;
  - drop cancels and joins in under 1 s.
- App worker tests `interrupted_index_verification_readmits_and_recovers` and
  `mismatched_index_verification_fails_clearly_without_readmitting` exercise
  the retry and tombstone paths through `perform`.

## Remaining

- 4K long-GOP warm seek misses 80 ms by about 2.6×, because preroll decode
  dominates. A proxy tier (spec §16.4) is the remaining route. It needs:
  - a supervised encoder worker, since `deadpan-encode` is documented as
    render-process only and its v1 contract requires audio;
  - a rebuildable cache with atomic publication and a sidecar index;
  - a preview-only reader that never reaches export;
  - refinement to the Original after scrubbing settles, with a visible
    quality tier in the viewer.

  None of this exists yet.
- One verified copy instead of two at cold admission. This needs a store
  change.
- No visible "measuring" state.
  - A mismatch is shown as the picture error.
  - An interruption retries silently, once per request.
  - The tombstone holds the failed session's private copy and idle decoder
    until the project session changes.
- No test covers an in-band SPS that disables skipping mid-stream; the
  committed MP4 fixtures carry SPS only in the AVC configuration.
- Page-cache-cold seeks, physical display latency and lower tiers are
  unmeasured.
