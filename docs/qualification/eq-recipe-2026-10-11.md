# Equalizer recipe and response, 2026-10-11

The [equalizer processor](../EQUALIZER.md) now implements validated static
three-band tone gains and optional low/high cuts through the qualified finite
FIR adapter. The recipe preserves exact authored values; one symmetric kernel
combines all bands and cuts without growing its fixed history or look-ahead.
Neutral EQ copies exact sample bits. There is no normalization, clipping or
channel crossfeed.

This is the numerical and parameter portion of specification §§10.2/17. No node
stores this recipe yet, and no native command exposes an unsupported treatment.
Retained-context, group, silence/tail and editor integration remain agent work.
DP-09 and DP-16 remain partial under the unchanged complete-product goal.

## Verification

All 1,051 core and 523 audio tests pass. The two new core tests cover typed and
JSON admission, exact round trips, neutral intent, finite gain bounds, integer
cutoff bounds, reversed/narrow passbands and unknown fields. Seven new audio
tests exercise the compiled coefficients and the actual native processor:

- Uniform gains compile to one exact scaled impulse; neutral PCM keeps signed
  zero, tiny values and full f64 precision.
- Three distinct tone gains reach their expected passbands with compensated
  phase. A combined 100 Hz/1 kHz/12 kHz input verifies signed stereo output at
  the original sample coordinates, with and without both cuts.
- Six cutoff choices include 80 Hz and 20 kHz. Measured cutoff amplitude is
  within 0.001 of 0.5; passband amplitude error and stopband amplitude 80 Hz away
  are below 0.0001. High-pass DC is below 1e-12. Paired high/low cuts sum to unity.
- The narrowest admitted 80 Hz passband retains its center within 0.002 of
  unity and suppresses the tested distant frequencies below 0.0001.
- All 108 combinations of extreme three-band gains and four cutoff patterns
  produce symmetric finite kernels accepted by the real native adapter.
- Four successive cores match fresh processors queried out of order exactly.
  Different recipes keep different kernels. Active and bypass paths reject
  invalid context, preserve both outputs on failure and honor cancellation.

These tolerances are sampled numerical assertions, not a claim of ideal
brick-wall filtering or perceptual acceptance. The implementation's transitions
are explicitly finite; cuts use approximately −6.02 dB transition centers.

The release `equalizer_probe` records coefficient hashes and complex frequency
responses for flat, three-band, speech-band, lowest/highest-cutoff and narrowest
passband recipes. The artifact binds the executed binary to its source and
toolchain. It evaluates the actual compiled coefficients; the PCM tests above
separately exercise native filtering. No device, GUI or encoded export is
involved in this response measurement.

The complete complex response samples and coefficient hashes remain in the
evidence. Workspace Clippy with all targets and `ui-harness`, formatting and
whitespace checks pass.

The primary agent reviewed the source and tests without subagents. Checked
parameter bounds, endpoint consolidation, cancellation/atomic publication,
unity bypass and fixed context, while preserving the explicit integration gap.
The first formatting attempt ran before the new test module existed and failed
to resolve that module; formatting passed once the file was written. No test or
DSP assertion was removed to pass qualification.
